# RESILIACT Project Webpage Plan

## 1. Purpose

Develop a credible, accessible, and actively maintained communication and dissemination website for the RESILIACT project.

The website should help five audiences quickly understand what RESILIACT does and find the material relevant to them:

- Researchers and technical partners
- Public authorities and civil-protection practitioners
- Policy makers and funders
- Communities, NGOs, and other stakeholders
- Journalists and the general public

The primary job of the site is not to describe the project in grant language. It is to make the problem, the project contribution, the people involved, and the practical results easy to understand and share.

## 2. Communication Objectives

1. Build recognition of the RESILIACT name and its purpose.
2. Explain the project challenge and approach in plain language.
3. Demonstrate progress through pilots, activities, outcomes, and people.
4. Make project outputs discoverable, downloadable, and reusable.
5. Create a dependable publication channel for news, events, and updates.
6. Support stakeholder engagement, newsletter sign-up, and contact requests.
7. Meet EU visibility, accessibility, privacy, and open-science expectations.

## 3. Reference Review

### TOGETHER: clear narrative plus regular activity

Reference: [together-euproject.eu](https://together-euproject.eu/)

Useful patterns to adopt:

- A long-form project name is paired with a short, plain-language explanation.
- The homepage moves from project purpose to a timeline and then to latest news.
- The footer provides a complete secondary navigation: About, News, Partners, Demonstrations and Living Labs, Events and Activities, Contact, and Materials.
- Newsletter subscription and social channels are visible as part of the communication loop.
- News is tied to real events, partner achievements, workshops, policy activity, and pilot work.

Implication for RESILIACT: the homepage should show evidence of movement, not only a static project description. The content model needs news, events, milestones, and outputs from the start.

### MYRIAD-EU: make complexity visible

Reference: [myriadproject.eu](https://www.myriadproject.eu/)

Useful patterns to adopt:

- The opening message frames a complex problem through a memorable idea: risks are interconnected.
- A pilot-region map creates an immediate route into the work.
- Pilot content is connected to sectors such as ecosystems, energy, finance, food, infrastructure, and tourism.
- News and events are separated, with clear archive links.
- A visible sign-up route gives visitors a reason to return.

Implication for RESILIACT: use a map, system diagram, or other visual index only when it explains the project. Each pilot or use case should answer: where is it, who is involved, what is being tested, and what has changed?

### PARATUS: validation note

Reference: [paratus-project.eu](https://www.paratus-project.eu/)

The site was rate-limited during automated retrieval, so its detailed page structure could not be independently verified for this plan. Before finalizing the information architecture, manually review its live navigation and note any useful patterns around project storytelling, results, pilots, and dissemination materials.

### NORSAR: research credibility and visual discipline

References: [NORSAR home](https://www.norsar.no/), [Projects](https://www.norsar.no/projects/), [About us](https://www.norsar.no/about-us/)

Useful patterns to adapt:

- A strong image-led page opening is followed by concise, authoritative copy.
- Content is grouped into clear institutional themes rather than crowded into one page.
- Projects are treated as a browsable portfolio with short descriptions and dedicated detail pages.
- News, impact stories, contact information, and social channels are easy to locate.
- The tone is factual, confident, and practical.

Implication for RESILIACT: use real project imagery and diagrams, restrained typography, short paragraphs, and evidence-based claims. Avoid a decorative or campaign-like visual language that could reduce trust.

## 5. Homepage Structure

### 1. Header

- RESILIACT logo and short project descriptor
- Primary navigation
- One high-value action: **Explore the project** or **See the pilots**
- Accessible mobile menu

### 2. Hero: the project in one breath

Use a real image from a pilot, field activity, monitoring site, or workshop. Pair it with:

- A short headline stating the change RESILIACT seeks to make
- A two-sentence explanation in non-technical language
- Links to **How it works** and **Explore the pilots**

Do not lead with the full funding call title, grant agreement number, or an unexplained acronym.

### 3. Why it matters

Three concise points covering the problem, who is affected, and why existing approaches are insufficient. Use a small number of factual metrics only when their source and date are available.

### 4. What RESILIACT does

Show the project approach as three to five connected steps, for example:

`Understand -> Co-design -> Test -> Evaluate -> Share`

Each step links to a short explanation and the relevant activity or work package.

### 5. Pilots and demonstrations

Use a visual map or structured cards. Every preview should show location, hazard or challenge, stakeholder group, and current status. Link to a detail page rather than placing all pilot copy on the homepage.

### 6. Impact pathway

Explain how research becomes usable change: evidence, tools, decisions, practice, and wider adoption. This section is especially important for policy and practitioner audiences.

### 7. Latest news and upcoming events

Show three recent news items and the next two events. Add filters or archive links on the full listing pages.

### 8. Featured result

Highlight one useful output at a time, such as a guide, publication, dataset, video, or policy brief. Include format, date, audience, and a direct download or view action.

### 9. Partners and participation

Show partner logos in a restrained grid, followed by a clear invitation to subscribe, participate, or contact the project.

### 10. Footer

Include EU funding acknowledgement, project identifier, contact email, newsletter link, social channels, privacy/cookie/accessibility links, and the full partner or legal information required by the grant agreement.

## 6. Content and Dissemination Model

### Content types

- **News:** progress, publications, partner stories, fieldwork, and external recognition.
- **Events:** date, location or online format, audience, registration, speakers, and follow-up material.
- **Results:** title, summary, audience, responsible activity, publication date, file type, language, and download link.
- **Pilot stories:** context, method, participants, status, evidence, lessons learned, and next step.
- **Explainers:** short pages translating technical concepts into accessible language.
- **Media:** photographs, diagrams, videos, logos, and approved social assets.

### Admin publishing area

The public webpage is view-only. Content creation and management happen in a protected admin area that is not exposed in the public navigation.

#### Roles and permissions

- **Administrator:** manage users and roles, site settings, navigation, taxonomy, media, and all content.
- **Editor or communications manager:** create, edit, schedule, publish, unpublish, and archive content; approve partner submissions.
- **Contributor:** create drafts and upload proposed media, but cannot publish directly.
- **Public visitor:** browse published content only; no account is required.

Use least-privilege permissions, strong password requirements, multi-factor authentication where available, session expiry, login rate limiting, audit logs, and a secure password-reset flow. Do not store passwords in the webpage application itself when an established identity provider can be used.

#### Publishing workflow

1. Contributor or editor creates a draft from a content template.
2. Draft is saved automatically and can be previewed without being public.
3. Editor reviews copy, links, images, accessibility fields, EU acknowledgement, and metadata.
4. Editor publishes immediately or schedules a publication date and time.
5. Published content can be updated, unpublished, or archived without deleting its URL history.

Show validation errors before publishing and record who created, reviewed, published, or changed an item. Add revision history and a restore option for published content.

#### Admin templates

Provide reusable forms with required and optional fields rather than a blank rich-text editor:

- **News:** title, standfirst, body, date, author or partner, topic, location, hero image, image credit, related project activity, external links, and social preview.
- **Event:** title, summary, start and end date/time, time zone, venue or online link, registration URL, speakers, audience, accessibility information, status, recording, and follow-up resources.
- **Result:** title, abstract, result type, audience, authoring partner, publication date, language, version, file type and size, DOI or external URL, related work package, and download link.
- **Pilot story:** location, challenge, participants, method, status, key evidence, gallery, map position, lessons learned, and next step.
- **Video or external resource:** title, description, provider, URL, thumbnail, duration, transcript or captions, and usage rights.

Templates should support draft, scheduled, published, and archived states. Use structured fields for dates, links, locations, and files so search, filtering, calendars, and related-content blocks work reliably.

### Interactive and dynamic experience

The webpage should feel active through purposeful motion and data-led interactions:

- Use CSS reveal animations for hero copy, section entrances, timeline milestones, and result cards.
- Animate only opacity and transforms where possible; avoid layout-shifting effects and continuous motion that competes with reading.
- Add a visible **Reduce motion** preference and honor `prefers-reduced-motion` by disabling non-essential animation.
- Include an interactive concept graph showing how RESILIACT connects challenges, stakeholders, methods, pilots, and results. Selecting a node should highlight related nodes and open a concise detail panel or page.
- Provide a non-interactive list or table equivalent for the concept graph so the same information works on mobile, with keyboard navigation, and with assistive technology.
- Make the pilot map or regional index filterable by location, challenge, stakeholder group, and status; provide a list view as a fallback.
- Add interactive timeline states for completed, active, and upcoming milestones, with accessible text labels and deep links.
- Use counters or chart animations only for sourced metrics, and show the exact value and source without requiring animation to interpret them.
- Embed YouTube or other approved external resources through a consent-aware component. Show the title, provider, thumbnail, duration, captions or transcript availability, and an external-link option before loading third-party content.
- Lazy-load video, maps, graphs, and large images; use responsive image formats and reserve dimensions to avoid cumulative layout shift.

Interactive elements should reveal project information, not function as decoration. Every animation and graph needs a static, readable fallback.

### Editorial rhythm

- Publish one meaningful update every two to four weeks during active project periods.
- Publish an event page before each activity and a short outcome story afterward.
- Review the homepage at least monthly so expired announcements do not dominate it.
- Maintain a quarterly content calendar tied to project milestones and reporting periods.
- Use consistent metadata: date, author or partner, topic, location, audience, and related output.

### Dissemination channels

- Website as the canonical source
- LinkedIn for professional and policy audiences
- YouTube or embedded video hosting for explainers and event recordings
- Newsletter for periodic summaries and calls to participate
- Partner websites and newsletters for amplification
- EU and sector networks for results and policy uptake

Every external post should link back to a durable webpage, not only to a social platform.

## 7. Visual and Brand Direction

### Design character

Use a NORSAR-inspired research identity: precise, calm, evidence-led, and connected to real places. The design should feel contemporary but not trendy, with strong photography, generous white space, clear diagrams, and confident headings.

### Starting palette

Treat these as implementation starting points and validate them against the approved RESILIACT and NORSAR assets before launch:

| Role          | Suggested color | Use                                               |
| ------------- | --------------- | ------------------------------------------------- |
| Deep navy     | `#082B45`       | Header, footer, primary text on light backgrounds |
| Research blue | `#006B9C`       | Links, buttons, diagrams, active states           |
| Signal cyan   | `#43B5C6`       | Data accents, highlights, map states              |
| Fjord green   | `#5D8C78`       | Nature, resilience, pilot or impact categories    |
| Warm amber    | `#E4A84B`       | Alerts, dates, featured results, limited emphasis |
| Off-white     | `#F4F6F4`       | Alternating sections and content backgrounds      |
| Charcoal      | `#17252B`       | Body text and high-contrast UI copy               |

Avoid using blue for every element. Green should communicate resilience or nature, amber should be reserved for attention, and color should never be the only way to convey status.

### Typography

- Select one distinctive but highly legible sans-serif for headings and one neutral sans-serif for body copy.
- Keep paragraphs short and use descriptive subheadings for scanning.
- Establish a type scale with strong contrast between page titles, section headings, labels, and body text.
- Do not place long all-caps grant language in hero-sized typography.

### Imagery

- Prioritize original fieldwork, workshops, equipment, landscapes, maps, and people using project results.
- Add descriptive alternative text and image credits.
- Do not use generic disaster imagery where a real project scene is available.
- Use diagrams to explain systems and workflows, not as decoration.

## 12. Version 1 CMS and Media Storage Recommendation

### Prototype-to-production boundary

The current Version 1 prototype uses browser `localStorage` so the publishing loop can be demonstrated without credentials or infrastructure. This is suitable only for local demonstrations: it is device-specific, not shared between administrators, not a secure authentication system, and cannot be used as the production CMS.

Before launch, replace the local adapter with a server-backed CMS API and a real identity provider. Keep the public website read-only and expose only published records through the public API.

### Recommended architecture

| Need                                               | Recommended option                                                               | Why                                                                                                                                                 |
| -------------------------------------------------- | -------------------------------------------------------------------------------- | --------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| News, events, results, pilots, users, workflow     | **Directus Cloud** or self-hosted Directus backed by PostgreSQL                  | Structured content types, roles, draft/review/publish states, REST/GraphQL API, media metadata, and an admin UI without building a CMS from scratch |
| Images, PDFs, diagrams, and downloadable documents | **Cloudflare R2** or **AWS S3** with a CDN                                       | Durable object storage, predictable public delivery, versioned files, and lower application-server load                                             |
| Video and event recordings                         | **YouTube** for public/open videos; **Vimeo** for branded or restricted playback | The project stores title, thumbnail, transcript/caption status, rights, and external URL only; large video files stay on the specialist platform    |
| Authentication                                     | NORSAR identity provider, Microsoft Entra ID, or CMS-managed MFA accounts        | Centralized access, role assignment, MFA, offboarding, and auditability                                                                             |
| Hosting and deployment                             | NORSAR-approved hosting or a managed frontend host such as Cloudflare Pages      | CDN delivery, HTTPS, preview deployments, and simple rollback                                                                                       |

Alternative managed options include **Sanity**, **Storyblok**, **Contentful**, or **Supabase**. Supabase is attractive when the team wants one managed PostgreSQL database, authentication, object storage, and a custom admin interface, but it requires more application development than Directus. A WordPress installation is also viable when the communications team already supports it, but it needs disciplined plugin, security, backup, and editorial maintenance.

### Storage rules

- Store structured content, not binary files, in the CMS database.
- Store original and web-optimized images in object storage; save the object key, alt text, credit, licence, focal point, dimensions, and checksum in CMS metadata.
- Store documents with explicit version, language, file type, file size, publication date, and access status.
- Keep video on YouTube or Vimeo and store only the approved canonical URL, embed URL, thumbnail, duration, transcript/caption link, rights, and consent state.
- Never upload a large video directly to the application server or commit media into the Git repository.
- Keep private drafts and unpublished media behind authenticated CMS storage. Use signed URLs for any non-public asset.
- Define retention, deletion, backup, and export procedures before collecting partner or participant data.

## 13. RESILIACT Hubs Map Improvement

Recorded on 7 October 2026 for future implementation. Scope: the map on the RESILIACT Hubs page and the matching homepage section.

### Recommended approach

Replace the current angular SVG geography with a refined atlas-style SVG map. Use detailed coastlines, pale blue sea, warm grey land, subtle country borders, carefully positioned labels and smooth selection transitions. Improving the underlying geographic outlines should take priority over colour changes alone.

This is the preferred approach for presenting the five Nordic hubs and six replication sites. Store the map data locally so the map remains lightweight and self-contained, with no map-service subscription required. Both locations on the website should use the same map design, selection behaviour and published Sanity content.

### Design and interaction

- Use [Natural Earth country boundaries](https://www.naturalearthdata.com/downloads/50m-cultural-vectors/50m-admin-0-countries-2/) with sufficient detail for Norway's coast and the islands. Choose and simplify the geometry for the final display size while retaining recognisable coastlines.
- Frame Europe and northern Africa so Iceland, the Nordic countries and Algiers remain comfortably visible.
- Represent Nordic disaster risk management innovation hubs with blue circles and replication sites with teal diamonds. Match the shapes and colours in the legend so the two groups remain distinguishable beyond colour alone.
- Highlight the selected location in orange, with a soft halo and a subtle tint on its country. Keep France unfilled because it has no project location.
- Give labels a light background for readability and adjust their placement to prevent overlap, including labels for replication sites. Retain Essex as the UK site name.
- Keep the existing location buttons and detail panel synchronized with selection on the map. Preserve keyboard access, mobile usability and reduced-motion support.
- Country shading indicates the selected location's country. Add precise pilot-area outlines only when those boundaries have been confirmed.

### Alternative for geographic exploration

An interactive vector map using [MapLibre GL JS](https://maplibre.org/maplibre-gl-js/docs/) could add zoom, pan and animated movement to the selected hub. This would suit future needs to explore local geography. Its map-data delivery and hosting requirements would need a separate assessment.

For the current overview of eleven project locations, the refined SVG approach is preferred because it supports polished geography and clear interaction without adding a map service.

### Completion criteria

Confirm coastline quality, geographic framing, label readability and legend clarity on desktop and mobile. Check all eleven locations, selected markers and country highlighting, keyboard interaction, reduced-motion behaviour and synchronization with the detail panel on both pages.

### Frontend setup sequence

1. **Confirm the hosting service.** Ask UiO whether Educloud supports this public project website or whether a different UiO web service is required. Agree on the endpoint, deployment method and persistent storage. Use Educloud project registration only if UiO confirms this is the appropriate service.
2. **Set up access.** Register or join the agreed project, obtain administrator approval and configure two-factor authentication. Use UiO's supported administrative route; do not assume public SSH or install services in a disposable desktop session.
3. **Build locally or in approved CI.** Install dependencies from the lockfile with a compatible Node version. On Windows, run `npm.cmd ci` followed by `npm.cmd run build`; on a Linux build runner use `npm ci` and `npm run build`. The deployment artifact is the complete root `dist/` directory. The production server does not need Node when it only serves these files. Vite's preview server is for local verification, not the production service. See [Vite deployment guidance](https://vite.dev/guide/static-deploy).
4. **Set production frontend configuration before building.** Use `VITE_SANITY_PROJECT_ID=l2s70ra1`, `VITE_SANITY_DATASET=production` and `VITE_SANITY_STUDIO_URL=https://resiliact-norsar.sanity.studio/`. These identify public services; do not put write tokens or passwords in any `VITE_*` variable.
5. **Deploy a staging release.** Copy the complete build to a versioned release directory through UiO's approved deployment mechanism. Have Nginx or the managed equivalent serve it. Keep a previous release for rollback, and retain assets needed by browsers that loaded an older release during deployment.
6. **Configure routes and caching.** Serve existing files and page directories, with `/index.html` as the fallback for event, dated blog and monthly archive paths. Missing asset files should return 404 rather than HTML. Use short or revalidated caching for HTML; cache fingerprinted build assets for longer. Do not give unversioned replacement images/videos immutable caching. Configure video MIME types and range-request support.
7. **Arrange DNS and HTTPS.** Have NORSAR point `resiliact.norsar.no` to the UiO-approved hostname or IP, using the record type UiO supplies. Confirm HTTPS certificate provisioning and renewal for that exact domain, either at a UiO reverse proxy or at the server. Redirect public HTTP to HTTPS. Test the public service from outside UiO's network without a university login.
8. **Validate and launch.** Test on the staging origin first, then the final domain. Deploy the verified release and record the owner, release, backup and rollback procedure.

Illustrative Nginx route handling, to adapt to UiO's approved configuration and web-root path:

```nginx
root /srv/resiliact/current;
index index.html;

location /assets/ {
    try_files $uri =404;
}

location / {
    try_files $uri $uri/ /index.html;
}
```

This fragment covers file and page routing only; TLS, access controls and logging follow the UiO hosting arrangement. Nginx documents the fallback behaviour in [try_files](https://nginx.org/en/docs/http/ngx_http_core_module.html#try_files).
